Abstract

AI4DRR is a hub to showcase experiments, prototypes, and proofs of concept exploring how Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) can support Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).

Our work combines practical experimentation with academic and methodological rigour, applying sound research methods, transparent approaches, and critical evaluation to explore new ways of understanding risk, strengthening preparedness, improving situational awareness, and supporting better decision-making before, during, and after disasters.

Mi-CAT

Mi-CAT is a machine-learning-integrated catastrophe modelling stack that aims to learn asset-level vulnerability from observed damage data, fuse fragmented multi-source data into harmonised hazard, exposure and loss bases, and couple the outputs with macro-fiscal models for budget planning and prevention prioritisation. Work so far has focused on predicting building damage grades after earthquakes.

DRR Chatbot

DRR Chatbot is an LLM-powered conversational assistant for disaster risk reduction, with the longer-term aim of interrogating disaster-loss data and generating visualisations with source citations. The current version answers questions from the UNDRR knowledge base, with every answer backed by citations.

Fonte

Fonte uses LLMs to extract disaster-event information from unstructured text and resolves affected locations into standardised geospatial geometries, producing structured, geolocated event records with less manual work.

Earthquake Meta-Model

Open global earthquake risk models can give very different loss estimates for the same country. This project explores how to combine models such as GEM and GIRI with historical loss records into a single loss curve with an explicit uncertainty band, as an input to fiscal risk analysis.

GENDRR

Persona-aware LLM approach tailoring retrieval, analysis and outputs to gender and inclusion needs, prioritising sex-, age- and disability-disaggregated information and grounded DRR guidance. More inclusive disaster-risk analysis, surfacing relevant disaggregated information and data gaps while producing grounded, role-specific outputs.
